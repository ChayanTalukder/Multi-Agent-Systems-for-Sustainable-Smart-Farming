# RQ2 — Communication, Coordination, and Shared-Resource Management

**RQ2:** *How do agricultural agents communicate, coordinate, cooperate, or negotiate when managing shared and limited farm resources?*

The main analysis uses **53 publications**, with an evidence-independence sensitivity check across **44 study families**.

## Source-coded interaction types

Interaction types are multi-label.

| Interaction | Original 34 | Final 53 | 44 families |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Coordination | 31 (91.2%) | 47 (88.7%) | 38 (86.4%) |
| Information sharing | 17 (50.0%) | 33 (62.3%) | 30 (68.2%) |
| Communication | 21 (61.8%) | 30 (56.6%) | 24 (54.5%) |
| Competition | 13 (38.2%) | 23 (43.4%) | 17 (38.6%) |
| Cooperation | 11 (32.4%) | 18 (34.0%) | 17 (38.6%) |
| Task allocation | 10 (29.4%) | 13 (24.5%) | 10 (22.7%) |
| Negotiation | 8 (23.5%) | 9 (17.0%) | 6 (13.6%) |

Communication, cooperation, competition, and coordination can occur simultaneously within the same system.

These themes are multi-label.

An auction or market is **not automatically an explicit bargaining protocol**.

Likewise, farmers interacting through a shared aquifer, canal, pasture, market, or environmental state should not automatically be described as communicating directly.

## Scarcity and constrained allocation

Explicit resource scarcity or operational constraints are represented in **50/53 publications (94.3%)** and **41/44 study families (93.2%)**.

The managed resources and tasks include:

- irrigation water;
- groundwater and reservoir capacity;
- pasture and land;
- energy and battery capacity;
- manure and nutrient capacity;
- machinery and robots;
- labour and harvest teams;
- agricultural tasks;
- crop-livestock exchanges.

Coordination objectives include efficiency, profitability, productivity, fairness, workload balance, environmental sustainability, conflict reduction, and multi-objective compromise.

## Communication mechanisms

Communication can take several forms:

- direct agent-to-agent messaging;
- peer-to-peer information exchange;
- bidding and market submission;
- shared state or blackboard mechanisms;
- communication through a central supervisor or auctioneer;
- indirect interaction through environmental or resource states.

The literature therefore does not support a single standard agricultural MAS communication protocol.

## Direct answer to RQ2

Agricultural MAS coordinate constrained decisions through a heterogeneous combination of direct communication, information sharing, hierarchical supervision, optimisation, task allocation, auctions and markets, peer diffusion, adaptive learning, explicit negotiation, and indirect shared-resource feedback.

Resource scarcity is almost universal in the corpus, but the mechanism used to handle scarcity depends strongly on the application.

Operational systems commonly use scheduling and optimisation, market-oriented systems use prices and bids, and socio-ecological models frequently represent coordination or competition indirectly through shared environmental and institutional states.

The study-family sensitivity analysis preserves these general patterns, indicating that the main conclusions are not driven solely by related publications.
